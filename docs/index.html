QVal: Evaluating Dense Supervision Signals for Long-Horizon LLM Agents
Authors: Sergio Hernández-Gutiérrez, Matteo Merler, Ilze Amanda Auzina, Joschka Strüber, Ameya Prabhu, Matthias Bethge
First: 2026-06-30T17:58:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-13T17:57:08+00:00
Comments: 10 pages, 6 figures in main text; 67 pages, 13 figures with appendix
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
LLM agents increasingly act over long horizons, where outcome-only rewards say little about which intermediate actions were good. Dense supervision methods address this by scoring intermediate steps, but they are usually evaluated through the downstream performance of a full training pipeline. This is expensive, confounds signal quality with other training choices, and means that methods are rarely compared on common ground. We introduce QVal, a training-free testbed that measures the $Q$-alignment of a dense signal: whether it orders the candidate actions in each state the same way as their returns under a reference policy do. We benchmark 21 methods from seven families with six open-weight backbones in four environments. Simple prompting baselines are the most $Q$-aligned on average, and performance clusters strongly by family. Code-generated signals do well only in small state and action spaces; embedding-based signals are weak; self-distillation signals are anti-aligned; and no method is $Q$-aligned in open-ended environments. When five of these signals guide an actor at test time by choosing among four proposed actions, $Q$-alignment strongly correlates with the gain in task success. QVal is designed to be easily extensible to new environments and methods, enabling researchers to compare new signals cheaply before committing to a training run.
Summary / 总结
LLM agents increasingly act over long horizons, where outcome-only rewards say little about which intermediate actions were good.
Envisioning Agentic Robotics: A New Era of Robotic Systems
Authors: Grid Robotics, :, Qingfeng Lan, Duo Li, Siyao Li, Yiheng Lyu, Shijian Lu, Zhenghao Qi, Shizun Wang, Xinchao Wang, Ming Yang, Zhiqin Yang, Zuhao Yang, Tao Yu
First: 2026-11-13T16:07:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-13T16:07:26+00:00
Comments: Project website: https://grid-robotics-official.github.io/agentic-robotics-roadmap
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
The release of GPT-6 Astra marks the emergence of a new paradigm in robotics: agentic robotics. Within weeks, frontier models with agentic harnesses matched or exceeded task-specific policies on many embodied benchmarks. Instead of training a dedicated policy for each family of tasks, an agentic robotic system applies a foundation model to open-world embodied tasks. It operates in a closed loop: observing the environment, interpreting user intent, reasoning about the task, invoking skills and tools, verifying outcomes, and adapting its strategy. Despite these advances, the community has yet to converge on a shared definition, a reliable evaluation framework, or a research roadmap. We argue that Embodied System 2, realized by a foundation model together with an embodied harness, forms the decision-making core of agentic robotics. Its central challenge is to translate general-purpose reasoning into effective and verifiable actions under physical constraints such as limited reaction time, mechanical limits, unpredictable environment changes, and irreversible consequences. Motivated by this perspective, we propose an evaluation framework that measures both model capabilities and closed-loop system performance. Furthermore, we identify the principles that carry over from digital to physical agents and the challenges that must be addressed to achieve reliable physical autonomy. On top of the Embodied System 2 and the evaluation framework, we envision a research roadmap toward sustained autonomy: the ability to perform work safely and reliably with progressively broader responsibility, from executing individual primitives to fulfilling standing roles.
Summary / 总结
The release of GPT-6 Astra marks the emergence of a new paradigm in robotics: agentic robotics.
PrivacyPR: A Dataset of Real-World Privacy Fixes for Benchmarking Privacy-Aware Code Review
Authors: Hua Yang, Aditya Bhardwaj, Shidong Pan, Bowen Xu
First: 2026-11-13T15:18:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-13T15:18:33+00:00
Comments: 4 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Coding agents increasingly review code, yet whether they catch the privacy defects that laws such as the GDPR require software to avoid remains largely untested, partly because few datasets connect real privacy defects to their accepted fixes and review context. We present PrivacyPR, a dataset of 4,131 merged GitHub pull requests (PRs) from 1,469 repositories, created between June 2018 and January 2024. Each PR includes its commits, diffs, review discussion, and LLM-generated labels for privacy relevance, fix type, and GDPR principles. PrivacyPR provides 1,169 high-confidence privacy fixes and 1,643 non-fix PRs, together with Verified, a manually checked split of 250 privacy fixes and 250 non-fix PRs, and Small, a 100 + 100 subset for evaluation. On Verified, both annotators endorsed 72% of the LLM's GDPR-principle assignments. Beyond supporting empirical study of how privacy defects arise and are fixed, PrivacyPR provides a benchmark for privacy-aware code review on real, developer-accepted fixes. Its evaluation harness reverts each fix to reintroduce the defect. On Small, two coding agents detect only 42-51% of defects under a generic review prompt, but 88-91% when explicitly asked to check privacy, while flagging 11-15% of non-fix PRs. These results show that privacy defects can be detected by coding agents, but that detection depends strongly on review intent and comes with false alarms. Data, code, reviews, judge verdicts, and an extensible evaluation harness are publicly available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23744765.

Coding agents increasingly review code, yet whether they catch the privacy defects that laws such as the GDPR require software to avoid remains largely untested, partly because few datasets connect real privacy defects to their accepted fixes and review context.
Proof-Grounded Patient-Specific Clinical Explanations from Knowledge-Graph Reasoning
Authors: Surajit Das
First: 2026-10-05T15:41:31+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-13T14:04:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Clinical decision support can lack auditable links from observations and encoded knowledge to conclusions and recommendations. Downstream of a frozen, training-free clinical knowledge-graph reasoner, the CKG Clinical Explanation Engine converts patient inference states and disease knowledge into typed facts, explicit rule traces, provenance-linked conclusions, and policy-licensed recommendations. Separate availability, representation completeness, and disease-specific activation distinguish observed zero-activation and partial evidence from unobserved evidence. Language-generation evaluation remains future work. Across six usable workbooks (11,289 patients, 33,867 patient-disease traces, 1,715,928 feature-evidence rows), IG-range validity, knowledge provenance, cross-sheet numerical fidelity (203,202/203,202), and exported fact/report field presence (33,867/33,867) reached 100.00%. Availability-representation consistency reached 99.29% (1,703,811/1,715,928), with 12,117 disagreements across five systematic feature-cohort patterns. Observed zero-activation and partial instances numbered 111,488 and 226,880, respectively. A separate seeded 30-patient end-to-end audit ran failure-free, passing all prespecified trace, licensing, provenance, and state-consistency checks. Paired comparisons (90 traces, fixed inherited diagnostic scores and policy catalogue) showed the availability-only baseline changed every report but no action sets. State collapse changed 20 action sets and removed 10 urgent-review occurrences. Rule replay reproduced all 40 action changes from reclassifying partial evidence as unobserved. The bounded validator detected all 450 single-fault mutants. Structural and implementation auditability were established. Clinical correctness and utility remain unestablished.
Summary / 总结
Clinical decision support can lack auditable links from observations and encoded knowledge to conclusions and recommendations.
CoffeeBench: Benchmarking Long-Horizon LLM Agents in Heterogeneous Multi-Agent Economies
Authors: Issa Sugiura, Daichi Hattori, Kazuo Araragi, Keita Ogawa, Shota Onose, Taro Makino, Teppei Usuki, Takashi Ishida
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-06-15T12:04:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-13T13:22:21+00:00
Comments: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026 (Evaluations and Datasets Track)
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
LLM agents are increasingly capable of handling complex, long-horizon tasks and may eventually take on business management, a role traditionally performed by humans. Deploying them safely requires monitoring their capabilities and behavior in environments that reflect real economies. We introduce CoffeeBench, a benchmark built on a compact simulation of a coffee supply chain with realistic business mechanics. Six autonomous LLM coding agents run its farmers, roasters, and retailers, and negotiate and trade with one another to maximize their own net income over 90 simulated days. We evaluate eight recent proprietary and open-weight models by letting each run one roaster while a fixed background model runs the other five firms, and scoring that roaster's net income. Claude Sonnet 5.5 earns the highest net income, closely followed by GPT-6 Astra. The leaders sell at higher prices, and the best model also buys its inputs far more cheaply, whereas weaker models trade at thin margins. Since performance targets are a common cause of accounting fraud in human firms, we also test whether agents under KPI pressure inflate their numbers. Under a revenue target, circular trades occur for some pairs of models: one agent proposes that the two rival firms sell goods back and forth at inflated prices so that both book revenue, and the rival accepts. We hope CoffeeBench serves as an early step toward a future in which LLM agents manage firms without misconduct. We release our code and agent trajectories.
Summary / 总结
LLM agents are increasingly capable of handling complex, long-horizon tasks and may eventually take on business management, a role traditionally performed by humans.
JFinBench: A Continuously Refreshable Japanese Financial Benchmark from the Latest Certification Exams
Authors: Issa Sugiura, Taro Makino, Takashi Ishida
First: 2026-11-13T12:59:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-13T12:59:12+00:00
Comments: 32 pages, 14 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly applied to finance, creating a need for benchmarks that evaluate their capabilities across financial systems with different regulations and practices. However, existing financial benchmarks provide limited coverage of country-specific financial systems, and they are difficult to keep up to date as released questions leak into training data. We introduce JFinBench, a Japanese financial benchmark comprising 321 examples from the 2026 editions of six regularly administered certification exams covering accounting, tax, financial planning, and banking. To construct it, we develop a human-in-the-loop agentic pipeline in which a coding agent writes exam-specific conversion code and a review page, while a human verifies every example and feeds corrections back to the agent. This pipeline adapts to heterogeneous exam formats and reduces the effort of incorporating new exam editions, enabling periodic refreshes that mitigate data contamination. Evaluating 12 recent LLMs, we find that Claude Fable 5 and GPT-6 Astra exceed 93% overall, while even the best open-weight model, Kimi K3.1 Pro, trails Claude Fable 5 by 12 percentage points. When models can additionally use a terminal and web access, they retrieve the knowledge they lack and every model improves. The gap narrows but about 4 points remain, and the ranking of models stays largely the same, suggesting that knowledge held in the model itself remains valuable even with tools. We release the dataset, the evaluation code, and the construction pipeline.
Summary / 总结
Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly applied to finance, creating a need for benchmarks that evaluate their capabilities across financial systems with different regulations and practices.
TelePhysica: Agentic Reconstruction of Physically Interactive Scenes
Authors: Ruiqi Shu, Xufeng Zhan, Yuanzhi Liang, Haibin Huang, Chi Zhang, Xuelong Li
First: 2026-11-13T12:02:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-13T12:02:43+00:00
Comments: Ongoing Work. Homepage: https://interactive4d.vercel.app/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Direct image-to-video diffusion models entangle pixel synthesis with implicit latent motion, precluding persistent 3D mechanical equilibrium, mid-rollout intervention, and counterfactual force control. Conversely, single-pass programmatic 3D reconstruction from an uncalibrated photograph leaves monocular depth and support clearance underconstrained, causing reconstructed scenes to interpenetrate or collapse under gravity. We present TelePhysica, a closed-loop framework that reconstructs an executable, multi-regime physical world from a single photograph to drive stateful 3D interaction and trajectory-grounded video synthesis. A vision-language coding agent synthesizes separable visual meshes and declarative physical models within a two-stage verification loop that pairs pixel-aligned silhouette critique with a zero-force gravitational equilibrium preflight. The verified world compiles into a unified solver coupling rigid bodies, articulated chains, co-dimensional cloth shells, and 3D tetrahedral elastodynamics, whose simulated rollouts condition pretrained video diffusion models through pixel-aligned structural streams. On a 100-scene single-image benchmark, closed-loop execution raises equilibrium-verified world recovery from $0\%$ in single-pass synthesis to $81\%$. Controlled constitutive ablations show over an order-of-magnitude larger localized corner displacement and out-of-plane pleating in finite-element cloth than in matched rigid-plate proxies. Conditioning video diffusion on simulated trajectories increases dynamic motion-mask overlap by $2{\times}\text{--}5{\times}$ and preserves motion direction under counterfactual force reversals where direct image-to-video models fail.
Summary / 总结
Direct image-to-video diffusion models entangle pixel synthesis with implicit latent motion, precluding persistent 3D mechanical equilibrium, mid-rollout intervention, and counterfactual force control.
BigCodeArena: Unveiling More Reliable Human Preferences in Code Generation via Execution
Authors: Terry Yue Zhuo, Xiaolong Jin, Hange Liu, Juyong Jiang, Tianyang Liu, Chen Gong, Bhupesh Bishnoi, Vaisakhi Mishra, Marek Suppa, Noah Ziems, Saiteja Utpala, Ming Xu, Guangyu Song, Kaixin Li, Yuhan Cao, Bo Liu, Zheng Liu, Sabina Abdurakhmanova, Wenhao Yu, Mengzhao Jia, Jihan Yao, Kenneth Hamilton, Kumar Shridhar, Minh Chien Vu, Dingmin Wang, Jiawei Liu, Zijian Wang, Qian Liu, Binyuan Hui, Meg Risdal, Ahsen Khaliq, Atin Sood, Zhenchang Xing, Wasi Uddin Ahmad, John Grundy, David Lo, Banghua Zhu, Xiaoning Du, Torsten Scholak, Leandro von Werra

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Crowdsourced model evaluation platforms, such as Chatbot Arena, enable real-time evaluation from human perspectives to assess the quality of model responses. In the coding domain, manually examining the quality of LLM-generated content is extremely challenging, as it requires understanding long chunks of raw code and deliberately simulating code execution. To this end, we introduce BigCodeArena, an open human evaluation platform for code generation backed by a comprehensive and on-the-fly execution environment. Built on top of Chatbot Arena, BigCodeArena enables the execution of LLM-generated code and allows humans to interact with the execution process and outcomes. We collected over 14,000 raw code-centric conversation sessions across 10 widely used LLMs, spanning 10 languages and 8 types of execution environments. Among these conversations, we identified more than 4,700 multi-turn samples with pairwise human preferences. Further analysis uncovers underexplored preferences of LLMs in fine-grained domains characterized by tasks, languages, and frameworks. To systematically examine code understanding and generation capabilities of frontier LLMs, we curated two benchmarks based on the collected data, namely BigCodeReward and AutoCodeArena. For BigCodeReward, we post-processed the 4,700 conversations and evaluated the consistency between reward models and human preferences. The evaluation shows that most LLMs have superior performance in judging coding preferences when the execution results are available. Inspired by these findings, we propose AutoCodeArena, an automatic Elo rating benchmark designed to assess the coding quality of LLMs without human involvement. We find that proprietary LLMs like GPT-5, Claude-Sonnet-4, and Claude-Opus-4 still lead in code generation performance among recent emerging models.
Summary / 总结
Crowdsourced model evaluation platforms, such as Chatbot Arena, enable real-time evaluation from human perspectives to assess the quality of model responses.
BigCodeBench: Benchmarking Code Generation with Diverse Function Calls and Complex Instructions
Authors: Terry Yue Zhuo, Minh Chien Vu, Jenny Chim, Han Hu, Wenhao Yu, Ratnadira Widyasari, Imam Nur Bani Yusuf, Haolan Zhan, Junda He, Indraneil Paul, Simon Brunner, Chen Gong, Thong Hoang, Armel Randy Zebaze, Xiaoheng Hong, Wen-Ding Li, Jean Kaddour, Ming Xu, Zhihan Zhang, Prateek Yadav, Naman Jain, Alex Gu, Zhoujun Cheng, Jiawei Liu, Qian Liu, Zijian Wang, Binyuan Hui, Niklas Muennighoff, David Lo, Daniel Fried, Xiaoning Du, Harm de Vries, Leandro Von Werra

Abstract
Task automation has been greatly empowered by the recent advances in Large Language Models (LLMs) via Python code, where the tasks ranging from software engineering development to general-purpose reasoning. While current benchmarks have shown that LLMs can solve tasks using programs like human developers, the majority of their evaluations are limited to short and self-contained algorithmic tasks or standalone function calls. Solving challenging and practical tasks requires the capability of utilizing diverse function calls as tools to efficiently implement functionalities like data analysis and web development. In addition, using multiple tools to solve a task needs compositional reasoning by accurately understanding complex instructions. Fulfilling both of these characteristics can pose a great challenge for LLMs.To assess how well LLMs can solve challenging and practical tasks via programs, we introduce BigCodeBench, a benchmark that challenges LLMs to invoke multiple function calls as tools from 139 libraries and 7 domains for 1,140 fine-grained tasks. To evaluate LLMs rigorously, each task encompasses 5.6 test cases with an average branch coverage of 99%. In addition, we propose a natural-language-oriented variant of BigCodeBench, BigCodeBench-Instruct, that automatically transforms the original docstrings into short instructions only with essential information. Our extensive evaluation of 60 LLMs shows that LLMs are not yet capable of following complex instructions to use function calls precisely, with scores up to 60%, significantly lower than the human performance of 97%. The results underscore the need for further advancements in this area.

Astraios: Parameter-Efficient Instruction Tuning Code Large Language Models
Authors: Terry Yue Zhuo, Armel Zebaze, Nitchakarn Suppattarachai, Leandro von Werra, Harm de Vries, Qian Liu, Niklas Muennighoff
First: 2024-01-01T15:30:19+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-13T11:35:07+00:00
Comments: 25 pages (12 main), 19 figures, 8 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The high cost of full-parameter fine-tuning (FFT) of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to a series of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods. However, it remains unclear which methods provide the best cost-performance trade-off at different model scales. We introduce Astraios, a suite of 28 instruction-tuned OctoCoder models using 7 tuning methods and 4 model sizes up to 16 billion parameters. Through investigations across 5 tasks and 8 different datasets encompassing both code comprehension and code generation tasks, we find that FFT generally leads to the best downstream performance across all scales, and PEFT methods differ significantly in their efficacy based on the model scale. LoRA usually offers the most favorable trade-off between cost and performance. Further investigation into the effects of these methods on both model robustness and code security reveals that larger models tend to demonstrate reduced robustness and less security. At last, we explore the relationships among updated parameters, cross-entropy loss, and task performance. We find that the tuning effectiveness observed in small models generalizes well to larger models, and the validation loss in instruction tuning can be a reliable indicator of overall downstream performance.
Summary / 总结
The high cost of full-parameter fine-tuning (FFT) of Large Language Models (LLMs) has led to a series of parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) methods.
PerfArena: Ranking LLMs by Code Performance
Authors: Rui Rua, Hailemariam Mersha, Joao Paulo Fernandes, Karim Ali
First: 2026-11-13T10:58:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-13T10:58:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Industry now relies on LLMs to write a large share of production code. Although that code is mostly, but not always, correct, correctness alone does not determine whether it is fit for repeated and extensive use, which depends on what the code costs in time, memory and energy. Most code-generation benchmarks nonetheless evaluate correctness in isolation, and most performance-oriented benchmarks cover few languages and rarely measure energy consumption. To address this gap, we present PERFARENA, an open-source, reproducible arena that ranks LLMs by the performance of their generated code across multiple languages and competitions. PERFARENA executes the solutions of each LLM on a Unix-like host and reports correctness together with latency, peak memory, and energy consumption. Correctness oracles, performance oracles, profilers, and code-generation workflows are pluggable in PERFARENA, enabling the integration of new judging backends and measurement tools without changing the pipeline. We evaluate PERFARENA on two competitions: CLBGComp, 10 compute-intensive benchmarks from the Computer Language Benchmarks Game, and LeetCodeComp, 99 LeetCode problems whose accepted solutions differ measurably in latency. Currently, PERFARENA holds 9,455 correct and measured solutions from 13 state-of-the-art LLMs across 7 programming languages. Across both competitions, got-6-astra is the only LLM that no other LLM beats significantly in latency, energy consumption, or peak memory, solving 99.7% of LeetCodeComp and 100% of CLBGComp. All PERFARENA leaderboards and measured solutions are publicly available, providing a continuously updated reference that helps developers choose an LLM per language and metric, instead of relying only on correctness rates or pricing.
Summary / 总结
Industry now relies on LLMs to write a large share of production code.
