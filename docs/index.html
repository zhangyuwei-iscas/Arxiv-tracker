Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Controllable real-world video generation models can condition on a rendered graybox proxy video, which fixes layout, motion and camera. Coding agents can build such videos by generating a 4D scene in Blender, yet no benchmark checks both these rendered proxy videos and the complex, multi-shot scenes behind them. We introduce VibePrevisBench, a benchmark for 4D scene generation: an agent turns a brief into an executable 4D scene and renders its proxy video. Its 242 tasks span four input kinds, including scripts, photos, continuations, and reference videos. Each delivery is scored along two evaluation dimensions. Rule-based Scene Check re-runs the code and compares the exported 4D scene with hidden ground truth facts in nine dimensions. VLM-based Screen Check asks a VLM judge whether facts that pass the rules are recognizable in the proxy video. We analyze the evaluation results and trajectories of 11 frontier coding agents and identify three key observations. First, multimodal understanding separates the agents, especially in understanding space and camera motion. Second, all agents seem to already master basic camera work and staging, and fail at challenging requests. Third, agents inspect their own renders but fail to correct their errors. Agents thus need better visual understanding of space and camera, explicit checks for challenging requests, and item-by-item verification of renders against the brief.

Agentic Harness Engineering Beta: Requirement-Centered Evolution of Agent Harnesses
Authors: Jiahang Lin, Jiahao Chen, Yiding Wang, Shichun Liu, Chengjun Pan, Shihan Dou, Honglin Guo, Yuyang Hu, Changhao Jiang, Zhenhua Han, Hang Yan, Qi Zhang, Tao Gui, Xuanjing Huang

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Real-world harness engineering is driven by explicit and implicit user requirements, which guide development and validation. However, existing harness evolution methods often overlook implicit requirements and interaction needs, while using a single evolution agent to modify the harness and benchmark scores to select changes. This process often produces narrow patches and leads to benchmark-specific local optima. We introduce Agentic Harness Engineering Beta (AHE-Beta), a requirement-centered loop that brings real-world engineering practices into harness evolution. The loop consists of three steps: (1) User-driven evaluation lets simulated users raise their requirements as issues while they work with the harness over multiple turns. (2) Team-based modification implements those requirements as generalizable changes rather than task-specific patches. (3) Requirement-driven selection merges a change only when it resolves a verified requirement and passes review. Empirically, on held-out tasks, AHE-Beta raises pass@1 from 21.9% to 33.3% while using 40.8% fewer tokens than Base Harness, and achieves higher pass@1 than Claude Code, Meta-Harness, and AHE. In a blind human A/B test, real users prefer AHE-Beta in 31 of 48 valid pairs and rate it higher on all five dimensions, matching the directional preference of the user simulator. The human A/B results show that AHE-Beta achieves its goal of aligning harness evolution with real-world user requirements. Without further evolution, the gains transfer to GLM-5.3 and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash with pass@1 improvements of 15.8% and 5.3% over Base Harness. Together, these results show that requirement-centered harness evolution is a more general method for improving agent capabilities.

Abstract
Large language models have been widely used to generate plausible research ideas, yet it remains unclear what makes automated ideation effective and how generated novel proposals arise from prior scientific work. We introduce the Creative AI Researcher (CAR), an agentic framework combining literature grounding, iterative development and explicit conceptual operators. CAR constructs a semantic knowledge graph from retrieved literature and equips an ideation agent with graph-retrieval tools and four operators: bisociation, analogy, exploration and elaboration. Each operator call is checked against structural acceptance conditions and recorded, producing a trace of intermediate transformations and declared literature sources. We evaluate CAR with two language-model backbones across 40 LiveIdeaBench research topics and two execution benchmarks. CAR achieves significantly higher methodological novelty (MN) than single-shot prompting and The AI Scientist. Its methodological novelty does not differ significantly from the Research Ideation Agent, while its feasibility scores are higher on both backbones. With a shared experiment executor, CAR achieves the highest pairwise win rate among the evaluated AI methods on FML-bench-Lite and BioXArena. In a ten-condition ablation study, MN point estimates favor CAR over one-step proposal on both backbones, but no MN contrast survives correction for multiple comparisons. The results do not establish a consistent quality benefit from explicit operators beyond free-form iteration. CAR makes the sequence of operations and declared literature sources inspectable, separating judged idea quality from procedural traceability.

CurvDelta: Low-Curvature Delta Modeling For Surgical Learning and Unlearning
Authors: Zeguan Xiao, Zhuocheng Liang, Yun Chen, Yong Wang, Guanhua Chen, Pinyan Lu

Abstract
A central challenge in adapting large language models (LLMs) is capability preservation: an adaptation can achieve its target while degrading general capabilities. We introduce CurvDelta, a scalable framework that incorporates the preservation constraint into the parameterization of the model update. By analyzing the local geometry of the preservation constraint around the pre-adaptation model, CurvDelta constructs a low-curvature space in which parameter updates leave the preservation loss nearly invariant. We make its construction practical for LLMs through Kronecker-factored approximate curvature (K-FAC) and a factorized projection. We also introduce CurvLoRA, a low-rank variant within the same preservation geometry, designed for more efficient adaptation. Both methods apply to preservation-constrained adaptation tasks such as capability learning and selective unlearning. We evaluate our methods on mathematical reasoning, code generation, and knowledge unlearning. For mathematical reasoning and code generation, our approaches achieve competitive downstream performance while better preserving general capabilities, offering a favorable acquisition--retention trade-off relative to the baselines. For knowledge unlearning, our main results on TOFU and MUSE show a better balance between targeted forgetting and knowledge retention than the baselines, with retention performance close to the from-scratch retraining reference.

Contour: Extensible Mapping Surfaces for Agentic Accelerator Kernel Generation
Authors: Ruijie Gao, Jirong Yang, Mengtian Hong, Youyou Lu, Barry Lyu, Haoran Jin, Nathan Bleier
First: 2026-08-01T23:22:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-18T15:55:25+00:00

Abstract
Emerging accelerators rarely ship with mature compiler backends, so recent work asks LLM agents to write native kernels directly from ISA documentation and simulator feedback. On two such accelerators, this direct workflow returns a correct kernel on only 17 of 38 workload instances, and most of its inference budget is spent re-deriving machine semantics and repairing illegal code rather than optimizing the workload. We present Contour, which consists of two parts. Contour-Explore replaces native-code generation with search over a mapping surface: a typed set of scheduling knobs and their legal domains, generated automatically from the accelerator's ISA description and the workload graph. A compiler-owned resolver completes partial assignments into legal plans and emits native code, so every evaluated candidate is verified against the compiler's default, and any policy can drive the search, including hill climbing, evolutionary search, and LLM agents. Compared with KernelCraft, a state-of-the-art agentic kernel generator, an LLM agent through Contour-Explore returns a verified kernel on every Gemmini and PLENA instance, produces 2.86x and 1.13x faster kernels on the instances where both succeed, and uses 26.3% and 83.4% fewer tokens per verified kernel. Contour-Ext extends the surface when search stalls: an LLM agent implements a new knob and its lowering in the compiler backend, and Contour-Ext accepts the extension only if the default path is byte-identical, every new value is numerically correct and changes the program, and the enlarged surface yields a measured gain. On VTA, Contour-Ext extends the AutoTVM surface of TVM, and AutoTVM's XGBTuner then finds 4.14x faster kernels; on PLENA, the extended surface makes the kernels found by hill climbing 1.20x faster.

Abstract
We present Piccolo, a family of multimodal decision models (0.8B to 27B) that coevolves model capabilities and training data via Agentic Data Engineering and recursive self-improvement (RSI). The models predict probabilities over candidate answers for general-purpose text and visual judgments, agent harnesses, data workflows, and interactive games. Piccolo-27B tops the Decision Index Vision Full leaderboard with a score of 75.59, while Piccolo-9B and Piccolo-4B achieve 68.35 and 67.46, respectively. All three rank first on the Private benchmark in their respective model-size categories. Research agents analyze model behavior, construct training data, conduct experiments, and use the results to refine subsequent proposals. Semantic slices and a shared research memory connect these actions across the model family's development. With training recipes comprising hundreds of thousands of examples, data selection and feedback-driven refinement are central to the self-improvement process. Our large-scale RSI exploration yields three counter-intuitive insights for decision-model development: (1) Backbone-recipe co-adaptation outweighs raw model recency: earlier backbones can surpass newer generations on visual tasks under identical recipes; (2) Targeted data curation enables 4B-9B models to rival 27B baselines on specialized workflows; and (3) Continuous loss reduction can decouple from and even degrade discrete decision accuracy. Together with the released weights and tools from the Data-Juicer and AgentScope ecosystems, Piccolo provides an actionable blueprint for building frontier decision models and scaling data-driven RSI.

Abstract
Mathematical proof assistants such as Coq, Agda, and Lean 4 build proof irrelevance into definitional conversion ($p \equiv q$ for $p, q : P$ with $P : Prop$), erasing which derivation established a theorem. While natural for mathematics over fixed axioms, judgmental proof irrelevance is incompatible with verified scientific stores, which must inspect and compare independent derivations of a claim to assess consilience and compute counterfactual retractions when an assay, reagent, or model is invalidated. Moreover, combining definitional proof irrelevance with subsingleton elimination breaks normalization and makes algorithmic conversion non-transitive. We present the type-theoretic and institutional foundations of Eigenius, a verified knowledge substrate for science. First, we formulate a dependent kernel with cumulative universes ($Prop, Set, Type_k$), impredicative $Prop$, inductive families with index-forced subsingleton elimination, and intensional identity types, where definitional equality $\equiv$ is untyped joinability under an orthogonal CRS and proof irrelevance is a witnessed axiom ($irrel : ΠP{:}Prop.\,Πp,q{:}P.\,Id\,P\,p\,q$). Second, we unify universe cumulativity, $Π$-covariant subtyping, and ontology class inclusions in a coercion-free subsumption judgment whose bidirectional fragments ($\mathcal{K}_0, \mathcal{K}_1$) satisfy subject reduction, compile to unification-free checkers ($\mathcal{A}_0, \mathcal{A}_1$), and match declarative typing. Third, we organize external verifiers into a three-level institutional hierarchy with explicit axiom policies (Adopt, Discharge, Relativize), internalize a justification logic with Why-provenance support $supp(j,P)$, and embed Cooper's Type Theory with Records (TTR) for open-world knowledge graphs. Finally, we prove consistency in Grothendieck universes and mechanize the metatheory in Lean 4 with zero sorry axioms.

Abstract
Multimodal knowledge graph reasoning (MKGR) aims to infer missing facts by jointly exploiting relational structures, textual descriptions, and visual evidence. Existing embedding-based methods effectively capture graph topology, but their fixed multimodal representations are less effective for query-specific fine-grained semantic reasoning, while generation-based methods are vulnerable to noisy retrieval and may produce invalid entities. To address these challenges, we propose NaviGATE, a noise-aware generative framework for MKGR. NaviGATE first aligns graph-derived structural representations with textual semantics, then performs structure-aware internal knowledge activation by fine-tuning the multimodal language model with multimodal entity summaries and injected structural tokens. In the noise-aware dual-view instruction tuning stage, NaviGATE jointly optimizes retrieval-free and retrieval-aware views: reliable retrieved candidates are reinforced through contrastive logit sharpening, while misleading candidates are suppressed through parametric consistency distillation. In inference, structure-constrained decoding restricts generation to valid entities in the target knowledge graph. Experiments on standard benchmarks show that NaviGATE consistently outperforms strong unimodal, multimodal, and generation-based baselines, and remains robust under noisy retrieval scenarios.

Abstract
Frontier coding agents increasingly exhibit overeagerness, taking surprising, sometimes reckless actions beyond a user's mandate --- reported in recent system cards from frontier model developers. These cases are surfaced through internal deployment monitoring, which requires private traffic and is out of reach for open-weight models and independent evaluators. Automated alignment audits are the natural alternative, but capable models increasingly recognise the synthetic setup and adjust their behaviour. Abstract

Abstract
Frontier language models can resolve repository-level software issues, but each attempt is expensive, and existing routers select a model from the issue text alone. We present SuperScout, which routes after scouting the repository: a 7B searcher, SuperScout-7B, first explores the repository and produces a structured handoff whose reproduction claims are sandbox-verified, with false claims stripped before delivery. Abstract

Most of the engineering on these agents goes into the harness, and vendors and leaderboards credit it with score gains. Abstract
