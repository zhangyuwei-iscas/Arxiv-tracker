RoboArchitect: Automating the Design of Robotic Agents with Persistent Memory
Authors: Shengguang Wu, Suning Huang, Cherie Ho, Ria Doshi, Hao Zhu, Serena Yeung-Levy
First: 2026-10-30T17:58:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:58:48+00:00
Comments: Project Website: https://roboarchitect-ai.github.io/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Recent work shows that a frozen robot policy performs far better inside an agent harness, where control code decides when to query the policy, consult vision tools, or command the arm directly. Yet today these harnesses are built by hand and redesigned for each new robot or benchmark. We show that harness design can be automated, offering a strong alternative to finetuning on a novel setup. In RoboArchitect, a coding model observes a robot's rollouts, diagnoses failures, and iteratively revises the harness around the frozen policy. The harness evolves on a set of episodes for its optimization and is evaluated on held-out scenes and tasks. Without updating any model weights, RoboArchitect raises average success from 9.7% to 43.3% on unseen scenes (4.5x) and from 12.7% to 40.2% on unseen tasks (3.2x), across multiple benchmarks and policies. The gains are largest where the benchmark is far from the policy's training data: wrapping a general-purpose policy, the evolved harness yields a specialist that often outperforms even the finetuned version. Intuitive control strategies are automatically designed, such as lifting a jammed arm clear or reaching in horizontally when an obstacle blocks the way from above. The harness also evolves its own persistent memory, carrying learned experience, such as effective grasp depths, into later decisions. These results suggest that substantial headroom in robotic manipulation lies in the agent harness, and its design can be effectively automated and optimized without weight updates.

Asynchronous Flow Map Language Models
Authors: Chanhyuk Lee, Wonjung Kim, Patrick Pynadath, Jaehoon Yoo, Satoshi Hayakawa, Ruqi Zhang, Jinwoo Kim, Seunghoon Hong
First: 2026-10-30T16:51:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T16:51:40+00:00
Comments: Code available at: https://github.com/david3684/async-fmlm
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
The order in which tokens are predicted in language modeling has a profound impact on the quality of generated sequences. Autoregressive and masked diffusion models iteratively fill in tokens and can exploit such orderings across generation steps, but how to leverage an ordering for one-step parallel language modeling is unclear. We introduce asynchronous flow map language models (aFMLMs), which incorporate prediction order into one-step modeling by learning the flow maps of asynchronous flows. These flows specify an ordering through a tokenwise time schedule that controls the relative denoising progress of tokens. We formulate a general class of asynchronous flows that admit both prescribed and learned, state-adaptive schedules, show that they define valid flow maps, and develop new methods for their flow map distillation. The resulting models can generate all tokens in a single forward pass by internalizing the ordering encoded in the flow into their hidden computations. On Sudoku solving and code generation for GSM8K math word problems, aFMLM establishes new state-of-the-art one-step performance.

I-HITL: Inalienable Human-in-the-Loop
Authors: Samuel Breckenridge, Dani Vilardell, Wenhao Wang, Fan Zhang, Ari Juels
First: 2026-10-26T17:52:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T16:40:31+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Human oversight is a pillar of AI safety. It is often exercised by a human in the loop (HITL) who approves critical actions taken by an AI agent. AI agents' increasing ability to bypass guardrails, though, raises the question of how to enforce HITL robustly. We introduce the concept of inalienable human-in-the-loop (I-HITL). An I-HITL protocol ensures that an agent can initiate an action only with explicit human approval, even when the human interacts with a system (e.g., a local agentic harness) compromised by the agent. A strong I-HITL protocol additionally ensures such approval even when an adversarial human attempts to delegate approvals to an agent. To motivate I-HITL, we report on experiments with a popular agentic wallet platform in which raising an agent's spending cap requires HITL approval via emailed one-time passcodes (OTPs). Given access to the user's email and a task blocked by the cap, we find that ten different frontier models will often autonomously raise the cap, harvesting OTPs to bypass a HITL guardrail and surfacing other vulnerabilities. We formally define I-HITL using the concept of communication topologies and prove necessary and sufficient conditions for its realization. We show that an I-HITL protocol requires a trusted entity - a trusted computing device (or equivalent) - and characterize which such devices can and cannot realize an I-HITL protocol. We present a prototype I-HITL system called Countersign that can plug into and strengthen existing HITL systems using off-the-shelf consumer mobile devices.
Summary / 总结
Human oversight is a pillar of AI safety.
RECAST: REconstruction of Chain-of-Thought via Auditable Structured Traces
Authors: Qi Wang, Kang Xu, Chengcheng Wan, Jiangtao Wang
First: 2026-10-30T14:36:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T14:36:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large closed-source reasoning models expose only their final answers. The intermediate reasoning that produces those answers is hidden behind API surfaces, tool-call policies, and safety filters. Existing chain-of-thought (CoT) extraction methods assume a side channel: a visible reasoning field, a scratchpad carried through tool calls, or a signed/encrypted reasoning block. We reproduce three representative state-of-the-art extraction methods and find that, on Claude-Sonnet-5.5 accessed through the standard chat-completions interface, all three return zero CoT tokens. The failure is structural rather than a bug: the reasoning field is absent, named tool selection is rejected, free-text scratchpad arguments are filtered, and no replayable reasoning block is exposed. We therefore take a different route. We present RECAST, a reconstruction-based framework that conditions a decoder on the question and the target model's final answer, reconstructs a first-person structured rationale, cleans it, and builds SFT-ready data. To the best of our knowledge, RECAST is the first system to reconstruct CoT from frontier closed models such as Claude-Sonnet-5.5, GPT-6.1, and Gemini 3 without using hidden reasoning channels. We instantiate RECAST on Claude-Sonnet-5.5 and additionally obtain successful qualitative reconstructions on GPT-6.1 and Gemini 3 with the same answer-only protocol. The system is open-source and packaged as an agent skill: one prompt deploys it and runs the complete pipeline to produce SFT-ready CoT data. This preprint describes the system, its threat model, and a controlled artifact validation; it does not claim large-scale benchmark improvements.
Summary / 总结
Large closed-source reasoning models expose only their final answers.
DeepWeave: Weaving Neural and Symbolic Evidence for ML-Based Network Intrusion Detection System
Authors: Raoul Saad, Enkeleda Bardhi, Andrea Agiollo, Fernando Kuipers
First: 2026-10-30T10:38:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T10:38:02+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Machine learning-based network intrusion detection systems can recognize complex traffic patterns, but often require substantial labelled attack data, degrade under deployment shift, and provide limited evidence for analysts. We present DeepWeave, a probabilistic neuro-symbolic Network Intrusion Detection System (NIDS) that makes logic an integral part of detection. DeepWeave automatically constructs and validates interpretable rules, represents neural predictions as probabilistic predicates, and reconciles both evidence sources through explicit coupling policies over packet-, flow-, and hybrid-views. The resulting proofs provide intrinsic explanations for rule-supported alerts. Across multiple datasets, DeepWeave improves low-data detection, false-positive control, transferability, and robustness. With only 50 labelled packet observations per attack class, it improves macro-F1 from 0.632 to 0.924 and reduces the attack false-positive rate from 0.075 to 0.008 over the matched neural baseline. Its proofs are also more stable than post-hoc neural attributions, while batched inference keeps probabilistic reasoning practical. These results demonstrate label-efficient, adaptable, and inspectable neuro-symbolic intrusion detection.
Summary / 总结
Machine learning-based network intrusion detection systems can recognize complex traffic patterns, but often require substantial labelled attack data, degrade under deployment shift, and provide limited evidence for analysts.
